What Is Creatine?

Creatine is a naturally occurring compound found in muscle cells. It helps produce adenosine triphosphate (ATP), the energy currency your body uses for high-intensity exercise.

Key points about creatine:

Naturally found in red meat and seafood

Stored as phosphocreatine in muscles

What Is Glutamine?

Glutamine is the most abundant amino acid in the bloodstream. It serves as fuel for rapidly dividing cells, notably those of your immune system and gut lining.

Key points about glutamine:

Found in protein-rich foods (meat, dairy, beans)

Typical supplemental dose: 5–10 g/day

Plays a critical role in maintaining the integrity of the intestinal barrier

Helps repair and regenerate the gut lining after stress or injury

Glutamine's gut benefits include:

Reducing intestinal permeability ("leaky gut")

Supporting immune function within the digestive tract

Feeding beneficial gut bacteria

Dosage and Timing

A common approach based on existing research:

• Creatine: – Loading phase (optional): 20 g/day split into 4 servings for 5–7 days – Maintenance: 3–5 g/day thereafter

• Glutamine: – 5–10 g/day, split between morning and post-workout

Tips for best absorption:

Mix supplements into water or a carb-containing beverage

Take creatine around workouts (pre or post)

Take glutamine on an empty stomach or with a small snack

Safety and Side Effects

Creatine and glutamine are generally safe for healthy individuals when used responsibly:

Rare side effects: stomach cramps, bloating (often solved by proper dosing)

Stay well-hydrated to support kidney function

Rare side effects: mild gastrointestinal discomfort at high doses

People with liver or kidney disease should consult a doctor first

Precautions:

If you experience severe abdominal pain, persistent diarrhea, blood in stool, or signs of dehydration, please seek medical help immediately.

Who Should Consider This Combo?

Ideal candidates:

Athletes seeking both muscle gains and digestive support

Individuals with mild digestive disturbances looking to strengthen their gut lining

Anyone under moderate training stress aiming for faster recovery

Not recommended for:

Pregnant or breastfeeding women without medical advice

People on medications affecting kidney or liver function

Those with serious gastrointestinal conditions (e.g., Crohn's disease) without a doctor's supervision

Practical Tips for Success
