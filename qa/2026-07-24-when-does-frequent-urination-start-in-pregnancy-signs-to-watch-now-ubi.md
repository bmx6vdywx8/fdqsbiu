When does frequent urination typically start during pregnancy?

[Signs to](https://repair-cascade.jcesqc8.com/)

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Frequent urination is one of the earliest signs of pregnancy, often beginning in the first trimester—sometimes as early as 4 to 6 weeks after conception. It's caused by hormonal changes (especially rising hCG) and increased blood flow to the kidneys. As pregnancy progresses into the third trimester, the growing uterus places added pressure on the bladder, which can cause the frequency to return or intensify.

Because frequent urination can also signal other conditions—like a urinary tract infection, diabetes, or overactive bladder—it's important not to assume the cause. Explanation

When you are pregnant, your body works very hard and changes a lot. One of these changes is that you might start needing to go to the bathroom more often. This change often begins in the first trimester, which means it can start just a few weeks after you become pregnant. Certain hormones make your kidneys work harder, so they make more urine. At the same time, your growing uterus puts extra pressure on your bladder, which can make you feel like you need to pee even when it is not full. As you get further along, especially in the third trimester, the pressure on your bladder can increase more, making you need to go even more often. It is a normal part of pregnancy, but sometimes it can be bothersome. If you have concerns about how often you are urinating or if you notice signs of an infection like burning during urination or fever, you can use a free symptom checker to help understand your symptoms and determine whether you should talk to your doctor. This way, you can be sure that you and your baby are healthy during your pregnancy.
