# Abdominal Bulge After DIEP Flap? Don’t Ignore These Warning Signs | Ubie Doctor's Note

Published on: 7/14/2026

What could cause an abdominal bulge after a DIEP flap procedure?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

An abdominal bulge after a DIEP flap procedure is typically caused by one of three issues: weakened abdominal muscles, separation of the rectus muscles (rectus diastasis), or a small hernia at the surgical site. While DIEP flap surgery preserves most abdominal muscle tissue, some patients still experience these complications during recovery. If you notice a new or growing bulge, it's important to consult your surgeon promptly to determine the underlying cause and appropriate treatment.

Explanation

After a DIEP flap breast reconstruction, tissue is taken from your lower belly to create the new breast. This surgery can sometimes weaken the muscles or change how they work. One common reason for an abdominal bulge is that the muscles in your belly may not be as strong after surgery. This can lead to a condition called rectus diastasis, where the muscles separate slightly, allowing a bulge to form. Another possibility is that a small hernia may develop, where a part of the internal tissue pushes out through a weakened spot in the muscle. These changes can make a bulge appear, especially when you stand, cough, or are active. Although this bulge might not always be dangerous, it can be uncomfortable and may need treatment if it causes pain or keeps growing. Your doctor may use physical exams or imaging tests like an ultrasound or CT scan to check the bulge. Depending on the findings, treatments can range from physical therapy and supportive garments to surgical repair if needed.
