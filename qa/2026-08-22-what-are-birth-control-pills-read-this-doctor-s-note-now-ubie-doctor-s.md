Birth control pills are daily oral medications used by women to prevent pregnancy. They contain hormones that work in two main ways: stopping the ovaries from releasing eggs (ovulation) and altering the lining of the uterus and cervical mucus to make it harder for sperm to reach an egg. When taken correctly, birth control pills are one of the most effective forms of contraception.

Explanation

Birth control pills, also known as oral contraceptives, are a type of medication used by women to prevent pregnancy. They usually contain hormones that stop the ovaries from releasing eggs. This is a key part of how they prevent pregnancy.

How They Work

Preventing Ovulation: The hormones in the pills stop the ovaries from releasing eggs.

Thickening Cervical Mucus: The pills make the mucus in the cervix thicker, which makes it harder for sperm to enter the uterus.

Thinning Uterine Lining: They also make the lining of the uterus thinner, which makes it less likely for a fertilized egg to attach.

Types of Birth Control Pills

Combined Pills: These have two hormones, estrogen and progestin, and are taken daily.
