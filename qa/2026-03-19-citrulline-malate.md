Natural Sources

Watermelon tops the list, offering about 1.5 grams of citrulline per 100 grams of fruit. Both the flesh and rind are good sources, and watermelon juice is an easy, refreshing way to increase intake.

Other foods like melons, cucumbers, and squash also provide small amounts of citrulline. While these won’t deliver performance-level doses, they contribute to overall amino acid balance in the body.

Supplementation and Dosage

For those seeking measurable performance benefits, citrulline is available in L-citrulline or citrulline malate forms. L-citrulline offers pure amino acid support, while citrulline malate adds malic acid, which enhances energy production.

A daily intake of 2 to 5 grams is commonly recommended for improving circulation, reducing fatigue, and supporting exercise performance. Some users may experience minor digestive discomfort at higher doses, but side effects are rare.

Combining food sources with supplementation can help ensure steady results for recovery, endurance, and vascular health—all without the need for synthetic performance enhancers.
