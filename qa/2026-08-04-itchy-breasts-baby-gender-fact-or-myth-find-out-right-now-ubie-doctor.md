Is there any evidence that itchy breasts during pregnancy can predict the baby's gender?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Itchy breasts during pregnancy do not predict your baby's gender. There is no scientific evidence linking breast itching to whether you're carrying a boy or a girl. Breast itchiness during pregnancy is typically caused by rapid skin stretching, hormonal changes, and increased blood flow as your body prepares for breastfeeding—not by fetal sex.

Explanation

Itchy breasts are a common symptom during pregnancy and are usually caused by hormonal changes and skin stretching as the body prepares for breastfeeding. These changes can lead to dryness, increased sensitivity, and itchiness in the breast area.

Some cultural beliefs and myths suggest that specific pregnancy symptoms—like itchy skin or how a person carries the baby—can reveal the baby's gender. However, medical research has not found any reliable link between these symptoms and fetal sex.
