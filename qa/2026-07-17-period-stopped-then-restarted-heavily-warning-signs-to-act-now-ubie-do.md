What does it mean if my period stopped and then restarted heavily?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Why did my period stop and start again with heavy bleeding? When your period stops and then returns with heavy bleeding, it usually means your body is shedding a thicker-than-normal uterine lining. This often happens due to hormonal imbalances, missed ovulation, thyroid issues, fibroids, polyps, stress, or perimenopause.

Common causes of a stop-and-start heavy period include:

Hormonal fluctuations (estrogen and progesterone imbalance)

Explanation

Your menstrual cycle works like a monthly process where the uterus builds up a lining and then sheds it during your period.

Sometimes, if your period stopped for a while and then came back very heavy, your body might have built up more lining than usual before shedding it all at once. This heavy bleeding can be due to hormonal imbalances, which might happen after a gap in your cycle, known as secondary amenorrhea.

Other factors like stress, significant weight changes, or issues with the uterus such as fibroids can also lead to heavy bleeding when your period returns. Sometimes after a break, the delicate balance of hormones can be a bit off, which makes the flow heavier than normal. Although this might not always be bad, it is important to observe any other symptoms like dizziness, weakness, or very large clots.
