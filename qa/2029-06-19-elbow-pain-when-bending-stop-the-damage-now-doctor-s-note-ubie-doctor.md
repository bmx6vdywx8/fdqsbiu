Elbow pain when bending is often caused by inflammation, strain, or injury to the tendons, ligaments, or other soft tissues that support the joint. Common causes include tendinitis (such as tennis or golfer's elbow), bursitis, sprains, arthritis, or nerve irritation. Overuse, repetitive motion, or a sudden injury can all trigger this discomfort.

Explanation

When you bend your elbow, the tendons and ligaments that help the joint move can become stressed. If a tendon or ligament is inflamed or injured—perhaps from overuse or a minor injury—it can hurt when you bend your arm. This type of pain can sometimes be seen in sports that put stress on the elbow, like throwing or pitching. For example, some athletes develop pain from tiny tears or strains in their tendons, which can hurt more during motion. In other cases, a previous elbow injury like a fracture may lead to discomfort when bending because the joint is not as stable. Other common reasons include tendinitis (inflammation of a tendon) or ligament sprain, which can cause pain when the joint moves.
