Can Too Much Vitamin B6 Actually Cause More Nerve Pain?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Excessive vitamin B6 intake can cause sensory neuropathy rather than relief. Symptoms include tingling, burning, or shooting pain in the hands and feet. Most cases involve supplement doses well above the 100 mg/day upper limit, often appearing after months or years of high-dose use.

Explanation

Too much vitamin B6 (pyridoxine) can indeed lead to nerve pain rather than relieving it. While vitamin B6 is essential for nerve function, muscle control and red blood cell formation, excessive intake—especially from supplements—can be harmful. Here's what you need to know about vitamin B6 toxicity and its effects on your nerves.

The Recommended Dietary Allowance (RDA) for adults is 1.3–1.7 mg/day, easily met through foods such as poultry, fish, potatoes, bananas and fortified cereals. Supplement users should be cautious: many over-the-counter B6 capsules provide 50–100 mg per dose—far above the RDA.

Understanding Vitamin B6 ToxicityExcessive vitamin B6 intake over time can lead to sensory neuropathy—damage to peripheral nerves causing pain, tingling or numbness. Key points:

• Tolerable Upper Intake Level (UL): 100 mg/day for adults• Onset of symptoms: often after months to years of high-dose supplementation• Most cases involve doses ≥ 200 mg/day, but some people report issues at 50–100 mg/day

Symptoms of B6-Induced Nerve DamageEarly warning signs of vitamin B6 toxicity often mimic other neuropathies. Watch for:

• Tingling or "pins and needles" in hands and feet• Burning sensations or sharp, shooting pains• Numbness or reduced coordination• Gait disturbances (difficulty walking)

These symptoms generally start in the toes and fingertips, then progress toward the trunk if intake continues unchecked.

Risk Factors for Vitamin B6 ToxicityNot everyone taking high doses will develop neuropathy. Factors that increase risk include:

• Long-term supplementation (months to years)• Daily doses above 100 mg for extended periods• Pre-existing nerve conditions (e.g., diabetic neuropathy)• Genetic differences in B6 metabolism

Even if you feel fine on high-dose B6, cumulative exposure can eventually trigger nerve damage. Latent toxicity means symptoms might appear long after you start supplementing.

Stop high-dose supplements immediately

Recovery and PrognosisMost individuals see partial or full recovery within weeks to months after discontinuing excessive B6. However:

• Some may experience lingering numbness or mild discomfort• Permanent damage is rare but possible with extremely high or prolonged doses• Reintroduction of B6 at normal levels (≤ 50 mg/day) is generally safe once symptoms resolve

Prevention Is KeyPreventing vitamin B6 toxicity is far easier than treating it. Follow these guidelines:

• Read supplement labels carefully. Note the milligram dose of B6.• Consult with a doctor or registered dietitian before starting high-dose supplements.• Report any tingling, burning or numbness to your healthcare provider immediately.• Balance B vitamins—overemphasis on one can disrupt the delicate interplay among all B vitamins.
