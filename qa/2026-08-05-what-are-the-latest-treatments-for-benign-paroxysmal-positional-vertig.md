Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) is most effectively treated with canalith repositioning maneuvers, a series of guided head and body movements designed to relocate displaced calcium crystals (otoconia) within the inner ear back to their proper position. The most common techniques include the Epley maneuver, the Semont maneuver, and the Brandt-Daroff exercises. These treatments are non-invasive, typically performed in a doctor's office, and often provide immediate relief after just one or two sessions. In cases where BPPV persists, vestibular rehabilitation therapy or, rarely, surgical options may be considered.

Because vertigo can stem from many different causes—ranging from inner ear issues to neurological conditions—understanding the root of your symptoms is the critical first step toward the right treatment. Benign Paroxysmal Positional Vertigo (BPPV) is a common cause of dizziness. The main treatment involves special head movements called repositioning maneuvers. These maneuvers help move small crystals in your ear that are causing the dizziness.

Repositioning Maneuvers

Epley Maneuver: This is the most common maneuver and involves a series of head movements to shift the crystals in the ear.

Semont Maneuver: Similar to Epley, but uses quicker movements.

Foster (Half-Somersault) Maneuver: This is a self-administered maneuver that can be done at home.

Follow-up: Sometimes, more than one session of maneuvers is needed.
