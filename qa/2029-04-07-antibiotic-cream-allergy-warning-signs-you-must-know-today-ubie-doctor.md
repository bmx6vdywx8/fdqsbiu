Can antibiotic creams cause allergies?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Yes, certain antibiotic creams can trigger allergic reactions. Bacitracin and neomycin are the most common culprits, often causing allergic contact dermatitis—symptoms include redness, itching, swelling, blistering, or a rash at the application site. In rare cases, these ingredients can cause anaphylaxis, a severe, life-threatening reaction requiring immediate medical attention.

Signs of an allergic reaction to antibiotic cream typically appear within hours to days of use. If you notice worsening irritation instead of healing, stop applying the cream and consult a healthcare provider.

Antibiotic creams are often used to prevent infections in minor cuts and wounds. However, some people can develop allergies to these creams. Bacitracin, a common ingredient in many topical antibiotic ointments, is known to cause allergic reactions. These reactions can range from mild skin irritation to severe allergic responses, like anaphylaxis, which is a serious and life-threatening condition.

Skin Reactions: Many people experience allergic contact dermatitis, which means their skin becomes red, itchy, and swollen after using the cream. This is because their body sees the cream as something harmful and reacts to it.

Severe Allergic Reactions: In rare cases, using bacitracin can cause a severe reaction called anaphylaxis. This needs immediate medical help, as it can make it hard to breathe and cause other serious problems.

If you notice any unusual skin changes or feel unwell after using an antibiotic cream, it's important to stop using it and talk to a healthcare professional. You can also use a free symptom checker to help assess your symptoms and determine if you need immediate medical attention. It's always good to be aware of how your body reacts to new products and to seek help if something doesn't feel right.
