# A-Rod: How I Lost 30 Pounds After a Surprising Diagnosis

This story is part of our ongoing “First Steps” series, where we share extraordinary stories of men who transformed their bodies, minds and lives with a focus on the first steps it took them to get there (because, after all, nothing can change without a first step!). Read all of the stories here.

ALEX RODRIGUEZ IS still best known for his time playing in Major League Baseball. The former Seattle Mariner, Texas Ranger, and New York Yankee had a resume that included 14 All-Star selections, 3 MVP awards, a 2009 World Series ring, and the fourth-most home runs in MLB history. But since hanging up his cleats for the final time in 2017, A-Rod has transformed his image off the field, becoming an omnipresent voice around the game of baseball as a broadcaster and an analyst, and also a prominent business figure.

Now, Alex is the Chairman and Chief Executive Officer of A-Rod Corp, where he invests in all sorts of start-up businesses, through which he's become part-owner of the NBA's Minnesota Timberwolves, and has even appeared as a Shark on Shark Tank.

Despite a thriving broadcasting and business career, though, not everything was going perfectly for A-Rod in his post-playing days. After a routine dentist visit led to a startling diagnosis, the former power hitter decided to make some major changes—which he explains in detail below.

IN MY YANKEE days, part of our medical routine was to go see the dentist twice a year, and so I've stuck with that ever since. During a recent visit to my dentist in Miami, I was diagnosed with periodontal disease, a chronic bacterial infection also known as gum disease, and that was shocking. I thought, Well, if people say you have a nice smile, you think that you’re free and clear. But the truth is that it doesn’t matter how it looks. Everybody is a potential victim, for lack of a better term.

That first step is always the hardest. For my overall health, that was starting with a focus on a very specific part of my day: from when I wake up in the morning until noon. I put that time, specifically, to my health and wellness—particularly my training and stretching. I do pilates, some yoga, along with steam, sauna, meditating. Then I get to the office at noon, and I’m there until 6, and then I come home and have dinner with my family. That routine has been one that is fairly new, and, so far, one that’s working quite well.

As far as my training goes, there are a bunch of things I did during my playing days that I don’t want to do anymore—primarily high-impact exercises like running, sprinting, and burpees, which are no longer necessary for my day-to-day life. But there are also a lot of similarities as well: a whole lot of stretching, some table work, and time in the sauna, stream room. The biggest difference is that I’m not lifting as hard, or as heavy, as I used to. These days, it’s more about mobility, flexibility, and staying healthy—not for performance.

When I looked at my overall health, I saw that I was was stretching myself out pretty thin. As a result, my health suffered, and over the last year, as I’ve taken control back, I’ve lost 32 pounds as a result. That wasn’t really the intention, but it was just the result of better habits: cutting back on meat; night walks; and consistent cardiovascular and weight training. I feel much better. I feel healthier, I feel younger, and I’m excited to attack each day.

Ever since making those changes, I’ve felt energized. I feel more clear in my thoughts. I’m moving around better. One benefit is that my clothes fit better—that part has been exciting.

At the beginning of my recent health journey, everything was hard; I felt like I wasn’t in a good place with my health. I felt sluggish, and a bit lethargic. Focusing in on that very first step—taking control of my mornings—will always be the hardest. But three months in, I started to see some results, and that was encouraging. After that, I just continued, with my head down, to be consistent with my health, my diet, and my training.

I’ve always been a big meat eater, and previously, I would eat steak sometimes five or six days a week. I’ve since cut that down, significantly, to only once every 10 days or so. Also, instead of working out two or three days a week, I’m trying to get into the gym six or seven days a week—even if it’s not crazy high intensity, just for the consistency. When I played for the Yankees, we played every day; it’s just about getting up, breaking a sweat, lifting some weights, doing some walks. Intermittent fasting has also been a night tool, especially when I travel. It’s easier to eat in that window of noon to 8:00 p.m..

Throughout it all, I always keep in touch with all my friends; I have relationships that go back 30, 40 years. I’m fortunate that I work with a couple of people that have been my best friends since we were nine years old. I have my work family, I have my family, and they kind of blend together a lot, because we spend so much time together. It’s been nice, and people ask me a lot of questions, and it looks like I’m inspiring some people. It’s good to share that story to try to help others; it’s always a really nice benefit.
