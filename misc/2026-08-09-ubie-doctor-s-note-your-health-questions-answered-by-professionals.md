Cardiac Arrest vs. Heart Attack: A Cardiologist Explains the Critical Difference

Heart attack vs. cardiac arrest: what's the difference? A **heart attack** is a *circulation problem*: a blocked coronary artery cuts off blood flow, damaging heart muscle. Treatment focuses on restoring blood flow with clot-busting medications or angioplasty. A **cardiac arrest** is an *electrical problem*: the heart's rhythm suddenly malfunctions and stops beating. Treatment requires immediate CPR and defibrillation within minutes to restart the heart. Key takeaway: A heart attack can trigger cardiac arrest, but they are not the same condition, and each requires a different emergency response. Central Sleep Apnea vs. Obstructive: How Sleep Specialists Tell Them Apart and Why Treatment Differs

Central sleep apnea (CSA) and obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) are two distinct disorders that disrupt breathing during sleep. CSA occurs when the brain fails to signal the breathing muscles, resulting in pauses with no breathing effort. OSA occurs when the throat collapses and blocks airflow, despite continued effort to breathe. **Key differences between CSA and OSA:** - **Cause:** CSA is neurological; OSA is mechanical (airway obstruction) - **Breathing effort:** Absent in CSA; present in OSA - **Common risk factors:** CSA is linked to heart failure, stroke, or opioid use; OSA is linked to obesity, large neck circumference, and airway anatomy **How doctors diagnose them:** - **Clinical history:** Symptoms, risk factors, and medical conditions - **Physical exam:** Airway anatomy and neurological signs - **Polysomnography (sleep study):** Determines whether breathing effort accompanies each pause **How treatments differ:** - **OSA:** CPAP, oral appliances, or weight management - **CSA:** Adaptive servo-ventilation or treating an underlying condition like heart failure or stroke Because symptoms overlap but causes and treatments diverge sharply, identifying which type you may have is the critical first step.
