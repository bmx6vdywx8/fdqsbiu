Explanation

Shilajit is a mineral-rich resin traditionally used in Ayurvedic medicine. What Is Shilajit?

Shilajit is a sticky, tar-like substance that oozes from rocks in high-altitude regions of the Himalayas, Altai, and Caucasus Mountains. Over centuries, plant and microbial matter are compressed and transformed into shilajit, which contains:

Fulvic and humic acids

Trace minerals (iron, magnesium, zinc, selenium)

Dibenzo-α-pyrones and related antioxidants

These constituents work together to support nutrient transport, cellular energy, and resilience to environmental stress.

How Shilajit Supports Oxygen Levels

Multiple mechanisms may explain how shilajit helps your body use oxygen more efficiently:

Mitochondrial Energy Production

Fulvic acid in shilajit aids the transport of electrons during cellular respiration.

Enhanced electron flow in mitochondria can boost ATP (energy) generation, making cells more efficient at consuming oxygen.

Antioxidant Protection

Dibenzopyrones and humic acids scavenge free radicals created when oxygen is used.

Reducing oxidative stress preserves mitochondrial integrity and supports healthier tissues, including lung and cardiovascular cells.

Iron-Rich Composition

Shilajit's trace iron may help maintain healthy hemoglobin levels.

Adequate hemoglobin is crucial for oxygen binding in red blood cells and distribution to organs.

Nitric Oxide Modulation

Some studies suggest shilajit enhances bioavailability of nitric oxide, a molecule that dilates blood vessels.

Improved blood flow can translate to better oxygen delivery in tissues.

Adaptation to Hypoxia

Research in high-altitude climbers indicates shilajit can reduce symptoms of altitude sickness.

By supporting oxygen utilization under low-oxygen conditions, shilajit may help the body adapt to intermittent hypoxia.

Sleep Apnea and Oxygen Deprivation

Obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) is a condition where the airway collapses repeatedly during sleep, causing:

Short pauses in breathing (apneas)

Drops in blood oxygen levels (intermittent hypoxia)

Fragmented sleep and daytime fatigue

Long-term risks: hypertension, heart disease, stroke

Enhanced Cellular Energy– Boosted mitochondrial function can help muscles involved in breathing (diaphragm, intercostals) work more efficiently.– Better energy production may translate to fewer fatigue-related breathing irregularities.

What the Research Says

A small clinical trial of high-altitude hikers found daily shilajit supplementation improved blood oxygen saturation and reduced fatigue.

Laboratory studies demonstrate that fulvic acid enhances oxygen uptake in cultured muscle cells.

No large-scale trials exist specifically in OSA patients, but overlapping mechanisms suggest potential for support.

How to Use Shilajit Safely

Before adding shilajit to your routine, consider these guidelines:

Dosage– Typical doses range from 300 mg to 500 mg per day of a purified shilajit extract.– Start with the lowest effective dose and monitor for any changes.

Quality and Purity– Choose products that are third-party tested for heavy metals and contaminants.– Look for standardized extracts guaranteeing fulvic acid content.

Timing– Take shilajit with meals to support nutrient absorption.– Avoid late-night dosing if you're sensitive to its energizing effects.

Potential Side Effects– Mild: headache, dizziness, upset stomach if taken on an empty stomach.– Rare: allergic reactions—discontinue use if you experience rash or difficulty breathing.

Drug Interactions– Low-quality shilajit may interact with blood thinners or diabetes medications.– Always review new supplements with your physician or pharmacist.

Should You Try It?

If you're actively recovering from sleep apnea and seeking natural ways to support oxygen levels and reduce oxidative stress, shilajit may be a helpful adjunct. However:

Always use it alongside—not in place of—prescribed OSA treatments such as CPAP or oral appliances.

Monitor your sleep quality and daytime energy; track any changes when starting shilajit.
