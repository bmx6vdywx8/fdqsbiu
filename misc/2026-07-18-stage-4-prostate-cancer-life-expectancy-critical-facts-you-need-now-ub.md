What is the life expectancy for stage 4 prostate cancer?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Stage 4 prostate cancer, or metastatic prostate cancer, has a median life expectancy of approximately 2 to 3 years after diagnosis. However, survival rates vary widely based on key factors such as overall health, cancer aggressiveness, PSA levels, treatment response, and where the cancer has spread. Many patients live significantly longer thanks to advances in hormone therapy, chemotherapy, immunotherapy, and targeted treatments. Understanding Stage 4 Prostate Cancer

Stage 4 prostate cancer indicates that the cancer has spread beyond the prostate gland to other parts of the body, such as bones, lymph nodes, or other organs. This stage is often associated with more severe symptoms and a poorer prognosis compared to earlier stages.

Survival Rates and Life Expectancy

Median Survival: Research indicates that the median survival for patients with stage 4 prostate cancer is around 2 to 3 years. However, this can vary widely among individuals (Siegel et al., 2020; Würnschimmel et al., 2021).
