# How to Handle a Groin Pull as a Lifter

Dr. Aaron Horschig, DPT, of Squat University has made it his mission to share helpful insights on how to decrease your pain from lifting. Whether you deal with back pain or knee pain, he's offered some great solutions. Now, he's sharing his best guidance on how to heal groin pulls or strains.

[How to Handle Groin](https://tissue-renewal.china-akan.com/blog/7263308108.html)

"The early stage of rehab for a groin pull (often a strain adductor longus muscle) will consist of three phases: relative rest, pain-free muscle activation, and soft tissue mobilization," says Dr. Horschig.

He also emphasize that you should not be stretching. But after a day or two of rest, you can start doing things to facilitate the healing process.

"Research shows that those who sustain a pulled groin often have a strength imbalance in the muscles that surround the hips. This is especially true of athletes who have weaker adductor muscles compared to the lateral hip or abductor muscles," says Dr. Horschig. "This means that improving the strength of the adductor muscles must be a priority in the prevention and post-injury rehab program."

To do this, he suggests activating the muscle via an isometric contraction.

To do this: Lay on your back with your knees bent, holding a small ball between your legs. Squeeze your knees together like you’re going to pop the ball. When you first start these exercises, think about contracting at about 50 to 75 percent of maximum effort—and don’t push through pain. Hold this contraction for five seconds before relaxing. As this becomes easier to perform, start squeezing with more intensity or increase the hold time to 10 to 15 seconds.

"Mobilizing the tissues of the inner thigh can also be a great help for relieving pain and promote proper healing of the injured groin," says Dr. Horschig.

To do this: Lie on your stomach with your injured leg positioned as close to 90 degrees from your torso as possible. Place the foam roller close to your groin, running perpendicular to your injured thigh.

"As your pain decreases and strength returns you can then start to slowly reincorporate your normal strength training into your plan." says Dr. Horschig.
