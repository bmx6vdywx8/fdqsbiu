What tests are used to diagnose excessive daytime sleepiness?

Reviewed by Yoshinori Abe, MD

Internal Medicine

Excessive daytime sleepiness (EDS) is typically diagnosed through a combination of clinical evaluation and specialized sleep studies. The most common diagnostic tool is the Multiple Sleep Latency Test (MSLT), which measures how quickly a person falls asleep in a quiet environment during the day. Doctors may also use polysomnography (an overnight sleep study), the Epworth Sleepiness Scale questionnaire, and monitor for microsleep episodes—brief, involuntary lapses into sleep that can signal an underlying disorder like narcolepsy, sleep apnea, or idiopathic hypersomnia.

Doctors use special tests to find out why someone is very tired during the day.

Common Tests

Multiple Sleep Latency Test (MSLT): This test measures how quickly a person falls asleep in a quiet environment during the day. People with excessive daytime sleepiness often fall asleep faster. This test is very good at finding out if someone is too fatigued.

Microsleep Episodes: Sometimes, doctors look for short periods where a person falls asleep without knowing it, called microsleep. This can help show if someone is too sleepy.
