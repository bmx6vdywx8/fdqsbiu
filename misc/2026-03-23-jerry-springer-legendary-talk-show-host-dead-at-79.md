# Jerry Springer, Legendary Talk Show Host, Dead at 79

Jerry Springer, host of the long-running and controversial talk show The Jerry Springer Show and the former mayor of Cincinnati, has died at 79, according to a statement from his family shared on WLWT.

"Jerry's ability to connect with people was at the heart of his success in everything he tried whether that was politics, broadcasting or just joking with people on the street who wanted a photo or a word," family spokesman Jene Galvin, Springer's lifelong friend, said in the statement. "He's irreplaceable and his loss hurts immensely, but memories of his intellect, heart and humor will live on."

Springer’s talk show aired for 27 seasons, debuting in 1991 and wrapping up in 2018 after 4,969 episodes. The syndicated talk show brought on guests embroiled in situations that could often be considered deviant—sexually or otherwise—and the show (which would find its host residing over the bizarre situations, judging the guests as his audience chanted, “JER-RY! JER-RY!”) even beat The Oprah Winfrey Show in the ratings in 1998 at the peak of its popularity, according to Variety.

His fame was inescapable in the cultural zeitgeist; with Jerry Springer so popular, the host played heightened versions of himself in numerous movies and TV shows, including the hit comedy Austin Powers: The Spy Who Shagged Me, Sabrina The Teenage Witch, and The Simpsons, among others.

Springer was always a man of multiple careers. Before his talk show fame he was a news anchor for WLWT; before that, he was a practicing attorney, and was even the Mayor of Cincinatti from 1977 to 1978.

In addition to his titular talk show, Springer was the host of America’s Got Talent for its second and third seasons (taking over for original host Regis Philbin), and between 2019 and 2022 hosted his own courtroom show called Judge Jerry.
