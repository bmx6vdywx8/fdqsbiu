Explanation

Varicocele—a dilation of veins in the scrotum—is a common finding in men and one of the most treatable causes of infertility. About 15% of all men and up to 40% of infertile men have a varicocele. If you're facing challenges conceiving, understanding how urologists evaluate varicocele and whether correcting it can boost fertility is key.

What Is a Varicocele?

A varicocele occurs when veins in the pampiniform plexus (a network of vessels in the spermatic cord) become enlarged or twisted.

It's similar to varicose veins that appear in the legs.

Most varicoceles develop on the left side due to anatomical factors; up to 10% of men have bilateral (both sides) varicoceles.

Varicoceles may be asymptomatic or cause mild scrotal discomfort, especially after standing or physical activity. Importantly, they can raise testicular temperature, impair blood flow and oxygen delivery, and lead to oxidative stress—factors that may damage sperm production.

How Urologists Evaluate Varicocele-Related Infertility

When men with varicocele seek fertility evaluation, urologists take a systematic approach:

Medical History and Physical Exam

Ask about fertility history, testicular pain or heaviness, past surgeries and lifestyle factors (smoking, heat exposure).

Perform a gentle scrotal exam—often with the patient standing—to feel for "bag of worms"–type veins.

Semen Analysis

The cornerstone of infertility workup.

Measures sperm concentration, motility (movement), morphology (shape) and total sperm count.

Ideally, two separate samples are tested at least two weeks apart.

Scrotal Ultrasound

Confirms varicocele and grades its severity (grades I–III based on vein diameter and reflux).

Helps identify other scrotal conditions, such as hydrocele or testicular tumors.

Hormone Testing

Evaluates testosterone, follicle-stimulating hormone (FSH) and luteinizing hormone (LH).

Identifies hormonal imbalances that can affect sperm production.

Partner Evaluation

Since fertility involves two people, a normal female partner evaluation ensures no other barriers to conception.

When urologists diagnose a clinically significant varicocele alongside abnormal semen parameters and no other female-factor issues, they discuss treatment options.

Treatment Options

Microsurgical Varicocelectomy

Gold standard: low recurrence and complication rates.

Laparoscopic or Open Surgery

Minimally invasive laparoscopy or a traditional open approach.

Generally effective but may carry a slightly higher risk of hydrocele (fluid buildup).

Percutaneous Embolization

Interventional radiology procedure.

A catheter is threaded into the spermatic vein, and coils or a sclerosant are used to block blood flow.

No surgical incision; quicker recovery, but success depends on the operator's experience.

Does Treatment Improve Fertility?

Improvements in Semen Parameters

Meta-analyses show that 60–75% of men experience improved sperm count, motility and morphology after varicocele repair.

Average increases range from 50% to 75% for total motile sperm count.

Natural Pregnancy Rates

After treatment, spontaneous pregnancy rates in couples range from 30% to 50% over 6–12 months.

Assisted Reproductive Technology (ART)

Men with persistent semen abnormalities may still proceed to IVF or ICSI.

Upgrading semen parameters through varicocele repair can reduce the need for invasive ART, lower costs and improve outcomes.

When to Speak to a Doctor

Prompt evaluation is key if you notice:

A lump or swelling in the scrotum.

Dull ache or heaviness in one or both testicles.

Difficulty conceiving after one year of unprotected intercourse (or six months if the female partner is over 35).

If you experience sudden, severe scrotal pain, fever or signs of infection, seek immediate medical care. Always speak to your doctor about any serious or persistent symptoms.
