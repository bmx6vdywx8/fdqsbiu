Thyroid health affects energy, mood, weight and overall wellness. If you take levothyroxine (brand name Synthroid) for hypothyroidism, adding herbal supplements like ashwagandha can seem attractive, especially since some claim it supports thyroid function. How Ashwagandha May Affect Thyroid Function

Several mechanisms have been proposed for how ashwagandha interacts with thyroid physiology:

Hormone Stimulation

Animal studies suggest ashwagandha can increase T4 (thyroxine) and T3 (triiodothyronine) levels.

In theory, this might boost thyroid activity—but it can also complicate dosing when you're already taking levothyroxine.

Absorption Interference

Levothyroxine requires consistent, empty‐stomach dosing for reliable absorption.

Any supplement that alters stomach pH or motility (including ashwagandha) risks reducing how much medication enters your bloodstream.

Ashwagandha may influence liver enzymes (cytochrome P450 system), which play a role in converting T4 to the active T3 form.

Altering these enzymes could affect how fast Synthroid is metabolized.

Individual Variability

People differ in gut health, genetics and overall metabolism.

What's mild for one person might be significant for another—making monitoring essential.

Clinical Evidence and Case Reports

While large human trials are limited, existing data raise caution:

A small randomized trial in subclinical hypothyroidism found that ashwagandha capsules (600 mg/day) improved thyroid hormone levels over 8 weeks. However, participants were not on thyroid medication, and safety long-term wasn't studied.

Case reports describe patients on levothyroxine who developed elevated thyroid levels (hyperthyroid symptoms) after starting high-dose ashwagandha.

Observational data indicate that herbal supplements can unpredictably alter thyroid tests, leading to dose adjustments and anxiety for patients and providers.

Why You Should Think Twice Before Combining Them

Risk of Over- or Under-Treatment

Increasing T4/T3 activity unintentionally could mimic hyperthyroidism: palpitations, anxiety, weight loss.

Reducing levothyroxine absorption could worsen hypothyroid symptoms: fatigue, weight gain, cold intolerance.

Lab Monitoring Confusion

Fluctuating thyroid-stimulating hormone (TSH) levels make it harder for your doctor to find the right Synthroid dose.

Frequent blood draws add cost and inconvenience.

Unknown Long-Term Safety

Best Practices if You're Considering Both

If you're curious about ashwagandha's potential benefits but already take Synthroid, follow these guidelines:

• Consult First Talk to an endocrinologist or your primary care provider before starting any new supplement.

• Space Them Out If approved by your doctor, take levothyroxine at least 60 minutes before breakfast and hold off on ashwagandha until mid-day or evening.

• Start Low, Go Slow Begin with the lowest possible ashwagandha dose and increase only under medical supervision.

• Monitor Labs Regularly Plan TSH, free T4 and free T3 checks 6–8 weeks after adding or changing any supplement.

• Watch for Symptoms Keep a log of any new palpitations, jitteriness, weight changes or energy shifts. Use this information to guide your doctor's decisions.
