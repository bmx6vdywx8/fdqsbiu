Lip Filler Techniques for Optimal Results

Professionals use a number of different lip filler techniques to make the lips look better. The method used depends on the patient's lip structure, the result they want, and how skilled the injector is.

Linear Threading Technique

In this method, the filler is injected along the lips in straight lines. It helps define the face and gives the lips shape without making them look too big.

Russian Lip Technique

This method makes your lips look raised and heart-shaped. The filler is put in a vertical position to make the Cupid's bow stand out and give the face a defined but natural look.

Tenting Technique

To add volume with this method, several vertical shots are used to keep the natural projection. It stops too much protrusion outward and is perfect for people who want a subtle improvement.

Microdroplet Technique

This technique involves injecting small amounts of filler at multiple points along the lips. It is particularly effective for refining shape and smoothing fine lines around the mouth.

Corner Lip Lift

The goal of this method is to lift the corners of the lips by carefully placing filler. In turn, it makes you look younger and more lifted.

Patients should expect little swelling, bruising, and pain following a lip filler procedure. Usually going away in a few days, these side effects are usual. Following the following aftercare advice will help patients to guarantee a good recovery and optimize outcomes:

For at least 24 hours avoid touching, stroking, or pressing on the lips.

To avoid aggravation throughout the first 24 hours, avoid alcohol, hot liquids, or straws.

Use cold packs sparingly to help with pain and swelling.

Sleep head raised to reduce nighttime puffiness.

Steer clear of heavy exercise for 24 to 48 hours to stop further blood flow that could aggravate bruising.

Maintaining lips moistened by a little lip balm and being hydrated will help.

Although improvements show right away after treatment, full results usually settle one to two weeks when any early swelling goes down. Patients should follow-up with their provider if they have asymmetry, chronic edema, or extreme pain.
