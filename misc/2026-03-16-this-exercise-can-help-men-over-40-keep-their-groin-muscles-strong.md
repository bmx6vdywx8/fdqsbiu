# This Exercise Can Help Men Over 40 Keep Their Groin Muscles Strong

Author, fitness model, and trainer Kirk Charles, NASM-CPT CES, knows that as you get older, life can get more complicated. But that shouldn’t prevent you from being on top of your game. He’ll help to answer the tough training questions that come with age so you too can be Fit Beyond 40.

[Help Men Over](https://defensecascade.china-akan.com/blog/6767407497.html)

About five years ago I played in a pickup basketball game with some friends. I hadn’t played consistently since my thirties. But, of course, my ego kicked in and I thought I could handle it. I’m personal trainer and I’m in “great” shape, right? What a rude awakening!

The next day I was expectedly sore, but my groin muscles seized up. I could barely walk for the next three days. I took a Pilates class that I had easily done many times before, but I barely got through it, and my groin muscles seized up again. Then another three days went by where I could barely walk. At that point I felt I had no choice but to pay special attention to my groin muscles.

My favorite bodyweight groin exercise is the Copenhagen plank (also known as the Copenhagen hip adduction). The movement is an advanced exercise that works your entire body. First, you will need raised platform, like a bench or chair, from which you can do an elevated side plank from your elbow. If you’re working your left adductor muscles, your right forearm will be grounded on the floor and your left foot would be grounded on the bench. Then you lift your body off the floor with your weight supported by your left foot and right forearm. This is the advanced version, which I do not recommend to get started with this exercise, since you can put a strain on the knee of the grounded foot. If you have pre-existing knee problems, totally stay away from this version.

A less difficult variation for beginners is to start with your raised knee bent at 90 degrees. This position helps to overcome any knee weaknesses or instabilities to perform the exercise. At this point your body should be on the floor, with your opposite knee bent as well. Again, your forearm is grounded on the floor. This is your starting position. Now you can lift your body off the floor, with your knee and forearm supporting your weight.

Your body should be in a straight line when you raise up to full height. Squeezing your glutes will help you maintain a straight line position. With your body off the floor, your adductor muscles should be fully engaged. You will also feel your upper body and core engaged. If you must bring your hips any higher to hold your position, it’s most likely because your core isn't strong enough. But, if that doesn’t work, I don’t recommend this exercise for you. You'll be better off strengthening your core with other exercises first.

I suggest holding your body in the elevated position for 10 seconds at a time to start. Once you’ve mastered that, extend the time incrementally to 30 seconds. Doing this exercise for 3 to 4 sets, three to four days a week, will certainly help to build stronger groin muscles.
