Inflammation Lab Test

The primary objective of an inflammation laboratory test is to assess the body’s inflammatory mechanisms. While these indications may not explicitly imply anemia, there are several methods to comprehend the relationship between inflammation and anemia.

C-Reactive Protein (CRP): Elevated CRP readings often suggest the presence of inflammation. Extended inflammation, characteristic of conditions like rheumatoid arthritis or chronic infections, can impact the production and lifespan of red blood cells, hence worsening anemia.

Elevated levels of the enzyme creatine kinase (CK) are commonly associated with muscle damage.2 Anemia can occur as a consequence of disorders that induce inflammation or muscle breakdown, which indirectly impacts the production of red blood cells.

Sedimentation Rate (Sed Rate): The sedimentation rate, also known as the red blood cell sedimentation rate, is a quantitative assessment of the rate at which red blood cells settle in a tube during a specific period of time. Although it is not a precise sign of inflammation, a continuous increase in levels of inflammation may suggest the presence of chronic inflammatory illnesses that might worsen anemia.

Elevated levels of homocysteine may be attributed to inflammation. Extended inflammation can impede the appropriate metabolism of folate, hence disrupting the production of red blood cells and leading to elevated levels of homocysteine.

Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH): LDH, an enzyme, is synthesized in response to cellular injury. Increased lipase levels may indicate tissue breakdown, including the disintegration of red blood cells. Chronic inflammatory diseases can contribute to increased LDH levels.
