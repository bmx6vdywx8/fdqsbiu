What is CBN?

CBN (short for cannabinol) is a nonpsychoactive cannabinoid.7 Cannabinoids, in general, are the biologically active compounds derived primarily from the Cannabis sativa plant.

Most early research on cannabinoids focused on the “major” or “big four” cannabinoids:9

THC (tetrahydrocannabinol)

CBD (cannabidiol)

CBG (cannabigerol)

CBC (cannabichromene)

But cannabis plants can produce more than 150 other compounds known as “minor cannabinoids,” which have only recently started to garner attention from the scientific community.9 CBN falls under this category.

Unlike other cannabinoids, CBN isn’t made by the plant; rather, it’s a product of THC degradation.8 This means that CBN concentrations are highest in aged cannabis products.9

How does CBN work?

CBN affects certain receptors in the body.20 They’re usually classed as either:21 22

Cannabinoid 1 receptors (CB1): Found throughout the body but primarily located in the brain and nervous system. As explained by Dr. Peter Grinspoon, MD, an instructor at Harvard Medical School, CB1 receptors “act like traffic cops to control the levels and activity of most of the other neurotransmitters.” As such, they can modulate or adjust physiological factors such as hunger, alertness, and body temperature as necessary.20

Cannabinoid 2 receptors (CB2): Mainly found in the body’s peripheral and immune tissues and the gastrointestinal system, but are also present to a lesser extent in the brain.23 CB2 receptors play a role in regulating eye pressure, gut motility, immune functioning, and intestinal inflammation, among other things.20 26 Researchers are also investigating the CB2 receptor as a “therapeutic target” for conditions like neuropathic pain and Alzheimer’s disease.23

CB1 and CB2 receptors are part of the body’s endocannabinoid system, which controls and regulates a wide range of functions (e.g., memory and learning, sleep, body temperature, emotion processing, pain response), and different cannabinoids affect these receptors in different ways.20

According to researchers, CBN has a “higher affinity” for CB2 receptors than for CB1 receptors, which is why it isn’t psychoactive.24 25 In contrast, the cannabinoid THC affects CB1 receptors more intensely (stimulating the CB1 receptors in the brain is what causes the high associated with THC).9 20

Having less of an effect on CB1 receptors doesn’t mean CBN has no neurological effect. On the contrary, the fact that CBN may improve various aspects of sleep suggests that it does have some effect; it’s just not as strong as one caused by some other cannabinoids.4 5

Is CBN legal?

At the time of this writing, yes. As long as the CBN is derived from hemp and has a THC concentration less than 0.3% on a dry weight basis, it’s generally legal to purchase and use in the United States.10

That said, because state laws vary, your access to certain CBN products (namely those that contain higher than 0.3% THC) will depend on where you live. The potential benefits of CBN

Research has shown that CBN has the potential to provide a wide range of benefits, including but not limited to sleep improvements, pain relief, anti-inflammatory effects, neuroprotection, and antimicrobial activity.4 7

The support for these benefits has mostly come from animal or cell studies or analyses of CBN’s chemical structure, so more high-quality human research is needed to confirm them. Moreover, Dr. Stanford notes: “It is important to remember that supplements are not regulated by the FDA. This means that the purity, potency, dose, and presence or absence of other ingredients aren’t guaranteed. Supplements have varying levels of third-party testing, as manufacturers can choose whether or not to invest in this step.”

Nevertheless, the existing literature on CBN is promising and warrants closer examination.

CBN to improve sleep

CBN is commonly described as the “sleepy cannabinoid in old weed,” indicating one of its most common uses: to improve sleep.9 Most CBN sleep studies are old (some date back to the 1970s) and altogether exhibit mixed results, but there have also been more recent, positive studies, including one conducted by Slumber, the manufacturer of our top CBN gummy.17

While Slumber’s study doesn’t appear to have been published in a journal, much less peer-reviewed, its findings are nonetheless valuable. In it, Slumber’s Deep Zzzs gummies helped 82% of participants stay asleep longer, 80% fall back asleep after waking, and 71% feel less anxiety, stress, and physical pain (all of which can affect sleep) after 21 days of use.27 Similarly, an unrelated 2023 study (possibly funded by Charlotte’s Web) found that 20mg of CBN isolate taken before bed reduced wakefulness throughout the night without worsening daytime sleepiness.5 Corroborating these studies’ findings, an independent 2025 review concluded that non-CBD cannabinoids like CBN can indeed improve sleep quality.54

CBN to relieve pain

In addition to an older 1975 study in mice that showed CBN could raise subjects' pain thresholds similar to aspirin,28 more recent research in rats has found that CBN, CBD, and both combined can reduce myofascial pain,11 including that from chronic muscle pain conditions, fibromyalgia, and temporomandibular disorders (e.g., TMJ issues).9 Myofascial pain syndrome is a chronic inflammatory condition that causes aching, throbbing, or tightness in the muscles. It can also include symptoms like trigger points, weakness, and a reduced range of motion. According to experts, up to 85% of the general population will experience myofascial pain at some point in their lives.49

All this research appears promising, but it’s important to temper your expectations. “Be sure to remember these results are based on animal testing,” says Dr. Stanford, “so the effects on humans may not align.”

CBN to reduce inflammation

The endocannabinoid system plays an important role in controlling your body’s inflammation response, and because of CBN’s greater affinity for CB2 receptors, it may act as an anti-inflammatory agent.7 9 In fact, in a study on human cells, researchers found that CBN inhibited various chemical messengers that can promote inflammation.7

Additionally, an older animal study from 1981 found that CBN may be useful for treating glaucoma due to its potential to prevent inflammation, which can raise eye pressure.29 A more recent glaucoma study from 2022 supports the findings of the older research, with the authors stating that CBN treatment may reduce the risk of blindness.30

CBN to protect the brain

In the aforementioned 2022 study, the researchers also explain that CBN appears to have neuroprotective effects and may prevent the degeneration of retinal ganglion cells (RGCs) — the cells damaged by glaucoma.30 31 The authors go on to state that the activation of CB2 receptors, in general, “has been linked to reduced inflammation and neuroprotection.”30

In a separate review, it’s noted that CBN has shown other neuroprotective abilities. These include inhibiting certain enzymes that could lead to neuronal damage and delaying the onset of amyotrophic lateral sclerosis (ALS) symptoms in mice with a SOD1 gene mutation.7 32 (Over 200 mutations of the SOD1 gene have been linked to ALS.)33

CBN as an antimicrobial

Who is CBN for?

Like other cannabinoids, CBN is intended for healthy, nonpregnant, non-breastfeeding adults ages 25 and up.

CBN is most often promoted as a way to help people sleep better throughout the night,5 but recent research suggests that it may also relieve muscle pain and inflammation.7 11 With at least one-third of U.S. adults reporting trouble sleeping,1 20.9% experiencing chronic pain,12 and 34.63% dealing with systemic inflammation, that means a good portion of the population could benefit from CBN.13

Who should look elsewhere?

Some people may wish (or need) to look beyond CBN for their health concerns, including but not limited to those who:14

Are pregnant or breastfeeding

Are under the age of 25

Take opioids, NSAIDs, benzodiazepines, or anticholinergic medications15

Have a family or personal history of psychotic disorders (e.g., schizophrenia)

Have liver conditions

Live in states that restrict certain types of CBN products

You should also look elsewhere if your sleep, pain, or inflammatory problems are chronic and severe. Despite the growing positive research, CBN isn’t likely to help with diagnoses such as insomnia and complex pain syndromes. A medical evaluation and possibly a prescription medication would be indicated instead.

If you’re interested in learning about some CBN alternatives, we detail a few options under the “Alternatives to CBN gummies” section toward the end of this guide.

Is CBN safe?

Like other cannabinoids, CBN should be generally safe for healthy, nonpregnant, non-breastfeeding adults aged 25 or older.14 But since CBN research is still very much in its infancy, it’s difficult to make a blanket yes-or-no statement about its safety, and even if you meet the inclusion criteria completely, you should always speak with your doctor before starting a new supplement.

Also, to reiterate, there are some people for whom CBN is contraindicated for safety reasons:14

People taking NSAIDs, opioids, benzodiazepines, or anticholinergic medications (CBN could interact with them)15

People with a family or personal history of psychotic disorders (CBN might exacerbate symptoms)

People with liver conditions (CBN may elevate certain liver enzymes)

It’s worth noting that these contraindications are for cannabinoids as a whole, not CBN exclusively. The research may eventually show that CBN has fewer exclusion criteria, but given the current scarcity of human research on CBN, you’d be better off playing it safe. As more high-quality studies are conducted and the scientific community’s knowledge of CBN and its safety grows, we’ll update this guide accordingly.
