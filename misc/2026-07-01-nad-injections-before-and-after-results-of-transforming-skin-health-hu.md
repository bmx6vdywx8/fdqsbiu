# NAD+ Injections Before and After Results of Transforming Skin Health | Hubmed

NAD+ (Nicotinamide Adenine Dinucleotide) is a vital coenzyme found in every cell of the human body, responsible for fueling metabolic reactions, DNA repair, and mitochondrial function. As we age, NAD+ levels decline, impacting energy production, cellular regeneration, and skin vitality.

In aesthetic medicine, NAD injections support systemic energy, optimize cellular communication, and create an internal environment where skin healing and vibrancy are naturally enhanced. Understanding the full scope of NAD+ injections before and after allows medical professionals to integrate this therapy into daily practice with precision and confidence.

Delivery Methods Explained: IM, SC, or IV?

NAD+ can be administered via intravenous (IV), intramuscular (IM), or subcutaneous (SC) injection. While IV is common in wellness clinics for systemic delivery, it requires longer appointments and clinical supervision.

IM and SC routes are increasingly favored due to ease of administration, shorter procedure time, and patient tolerance. IM injections offer rapid absorption and are often used when quick energy restoration is desired. SC injections, by contrast, allow for a slower, sustained release of NAD+, ideal for ongoing skin health and maintenance.

Who Should Consider NAD+ Injections?

Ideal candidates for NAD+ therapy in aesthetic medicine include patients struggling with fatigue, burnout, poor post-procedure healing, and signs of “tired skin.” It’s particularly beneficial for those recovering from illness, high-stress lifestyles, or experiencing an age-related slowdown in skin recovery and collagen synthesis.

Patients with dull, dehydrated, or inflamed skin, especially after procedures like laser or RF, may notice faster recovery and a return to glow. Injectable NAD results are most appreciated in patients who show delayed healing, loss of firmness, or chronic skin stress.

Before administering NAD+, ensure patients are hydrated and have provided a full medical history, especially regarding autoimmune disease, cancer history, or pregnancy.

Protocols typically include 4 to 8 weekly sessions, depending on clinical goals. Combination therapies are common, pairing NAD+ injections with exosomes, radiofrequency (RF) devices, or biostimulatory fillers to enhance skin regeneration.

When NAD+ Is Not Recommended

Although most individuals tolerate NAD+ injections without complications, the therapy is not universally appropriate and should be administered with patient selection. Careful screening is crucial to minimize risk and ensure optimal outcomes. This therapy should be avoided in individuals with the following conditions:

Pregnancy or breastfeeding.

Active autoimmune conditions.

Ongoing cancer treatment or history of malignancy.

Severe liver or kidney dysfunction.

Known hypersensitivity to NAD+ or any component of the formulation.

Uncontrolled hypertension or cardiac arrhythmias.

History of seizure disorders or epilepsy.

Current psychiatric instability or untreated anxiety disorders.

Recent cerebrovascular events, such as stroke or TIA.

Use of MAO inhibitors or stimulant-based medications without physician oversight.

Not directly. NAD+ improves skin function and collagen health, which may slightly tighten skin over time, but it doesn’t replace lifting procedures.

No. Ozempic is a GLP-1 receptor agonist for blood sugar and weight loss, while NAD+ is a cellular coenzyme used for energy metabolism and repair.
