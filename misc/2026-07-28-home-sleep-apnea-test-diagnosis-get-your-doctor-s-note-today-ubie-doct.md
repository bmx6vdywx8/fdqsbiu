Home sleep apnea tests offer a convenient way to diagnose obstructive sleep apnea from your own bed, typically using a portable device that monitors breathing, oxygen levels, and heart rate overnight. However, they are generally less accurate than in-lab polysomnography, which tracks a broader range of data including brain activity, sleep stages, and body movement. Home tests may underestimate the severity of sleep apnea or miss it entirely, especially in people with mild symptoms or other underlying sleep disorders. For this reason, a negative home test result doesn't always rule out sleep apnea, and follow-up lab testing may be recommended.
