The Rock is in China hyping his newest movie, Rampage, but an international press junket is just another excuse for him to show off his steadfast dedication to the weight room.

The man also known as Dwayne Johnson has posted a whole slew of workout pics on Instagram as he promotes the film, keeping his 103 million followers in the gym with him. In one of the photos, he's rocking a gym accessory we've seen him use before: a heavy ass chain.

The chains actually fit in nicely with Rampage's monster-filled plot, but that's not why Johnson busted them out in China. The heavy links are clearly one of his favorite weight room tools—they're littered all over his social media workouts.

Adding chains to otherwise normal lifts is an old school bodybuilding technique that has stood the test of time. The links can simply add extra weight in moves like dips and pullups, or make balancing a barbell more difficult as you account for the shifting links.

The most interesting aspect of chain training is the way it affects lifts like squats and bench press. As you proceed through the movement, the added weight fluctuates as more of the chain lowers and lifts from the floor below.

Just check out this video posted by the Rock himself to see how it works.

As he lowers the bar, more of the chain is on the ground, making the load lighter. When he presses the bar up, the links come up too, effectively "adding" to the overall weight. This allows you to handle more weight at your strongest point, the lockout, with less during the part of the lift where you're at your weakest.

Here are five moves that you can add chains to for a more Rock-like workout. Just remember: You're NOT the Rock. Only bring chains into the equation once you've mastered the most basic form of the exercise in question to keep your workout safe. That might take a while, and you might not get as big as he is, but eventually your weight room can sound just as clinky as his.

Weighted dips appear to be one of the Rock's go-to chain moves. Just drape the chain around your neck, and boom—you have a lot more to work with than just your body weight. You can lean your torso forward to target the chest, or keep your torso upright to focus on your triceps. Chains have the same effect as a belt with a weight attached or a weighted vest, but c'mon... you know which looks the coolest.

Back Barbell Squats

Chain barbell squats give you a perfect opportunity to add other variations to the basic lift. Try adding pauses at the bottom of the movement, or even sitting on a box before exploding up into the locked out position.

Bench Press

Like squats, the chain bench press offers good reason to think outside the box. You can try different techniques like the Rock (rewatch that video on form above for a second look), alternating between full reps, half reps, and pauses to work on your range.
