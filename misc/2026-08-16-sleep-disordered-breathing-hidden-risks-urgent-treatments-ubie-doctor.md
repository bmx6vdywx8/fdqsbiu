Sleep disordered breathing (SDB) refers to a group of conditions marked by abnormal breathing patterns during sleep, including snoring, shallow breathing, or pauses in breathing (apnea). Common types include obstructive sleep apnea, central sleep apnea, and upper airway resistance syndrome.

Treatment options depend on severity and cause, and may include:

Lifestyle changes: weight loss, sleeping on your side, avoiding alcohol before bed

Breathing devices: CPAP, BiPAP, or oral appliances

Surgery: to remove airway obstructions in select cases

Left untreated, SDB can raise your risk of heart disease, high blood pressure, and daytime fatigue. If you're experiencing symptoms like loud snoring, morning headaches, or excessive daytime sleepiness, understanding the cause is the critical first step. Sleep disordered breathing is a condition where a person has trouble breathing during sleep. This can include loud snoring or pauses in breathing, which are common signs of a condition called sleep apnea. These breathing problems can make it hard to get a good night's sleep and can affect how you feel during the day.

Treatment Options:

Lifestyle Changes:

Losing weight if needed

Sleeping on your side

Avoiding alcohol and smoking

Devices:

Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP): A mask worn over the nose or mouth that helps keep airways open.

Oral Appliances: Devices worn in the mouth to help keep the throat open.
