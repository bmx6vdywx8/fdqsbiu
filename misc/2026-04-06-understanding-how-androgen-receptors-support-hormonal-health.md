# Understanding How Androgen Receptors Support Hormonal Health

Reviewed by: Dr. Hal and Gameday's Medical Advisory Board

Androgen receptors (ARs) play a vital role in how the body responds to male sex hormones like testosterone and dihydrotestosterone (DHT). These receptors are involved in muscle, sexual health, and the regulation of metabolic processes.

In this article, we’ll discuss how androgen receptors work and how men can optimize their function to improve their overall health and well-being.

What Are Androgen Receptors?

Androgen receptors are proteins found throughout the body that bind to androgens like testosterone and DHT, triggering gene expressions that influence physiological processes. These receptors are essential for regulating male-specific functions such as muscle, sexual development, and overall metabolic health.

Defining Androgens and Their Receptors

Androgens such as testosterone and DHT are key male sex hormones that regulate numerous bodily functions, including muscle mass and libido. They can also affect things like body hair growth and voice change. (1)

Androgen receptors (ARs) are intracellular proteins that bind to these hormones, activating or suppressing gene expressions to influence various processes such as bone and muscle development, metabolism regulation, and sexual behavior. (2)

Where They’re Found

Androgen receptors are distributed throughout the body, with high concentrations in skeletal muscle tissue, reproductive organs, skin, and the brain. In muscle, these receptors mediate testosterone’s effects on protein synthesis and strength development, while in reproductive tissues, they regulate sexual function and fertility. Their presence in skin and hair follicles influences hair growth patterns, including male pattern baldness.

The brain contains androgen receptors in regions like the hypothalamus, amygdala, and prefrontal cortex, where they affect cognition, mood, and sexual behavior. Additionally, ARs are found in bone tissue (influencing density and growth), liver (regulating protein synthesis and metabolism), and adipose tissue (affecting fat distribution).

How Androgen Receptors Work

Once androgens bind to ARs, several events occur, resulting in changes inside the body, such as muscle development or sexual arousal. The sensitivity of androgen receptors can significantly affect how strongly an individual responds to testosterone and other hormones.

From Binding to Gene Expression

When androgens bind to androgen receptors, they enter cells and initiate the activation or suppression of specific genes. This process results in significant physiological changes, such as increased muscle protein synthesis, enhanced libido, and improved fat metabolism.

Essentially, ARs play a key role in turning on or off certain processes that shape physical and sexual health.

The Role of AR Sensitivity

The sensitivity of androgen receptors varies between individuals and can influence how effectively a person responds to testosterone. Genetic factors, lifestyle choices, and certain medical conditions can either enhance or reduce AR sensitivity, affecting how well hormones like testosterone are utilized in the body.

Exercise, a healthy diet and nutrition, limiting alcohol intake, and proper sleep and stress management can help better utilize testosterone and boost overall metabolic function. (3)

Factors Affecting Androgen Receptor Function

Several factors, including hormonal balance, lifestyle habits, and genetic predispositions, can influence androgen receptor activity. Understanding these factors helps men optimize their AR function and achieve better overall health.

Hormonal Balance and Co-Factors

Hormones like estrogen, SHBG (sex hormone-binding globulin), and vitamin D interact with androgen receptors to regulate their activity. (4) Adequate levels of micronutrients like zinc and magnesium also support optimal hormone signaling, ensuring androgen receptors function properly.

Lifestyle and Environmental Influences

Environmental and lifestyle factors can also negatively affect androgen receptor function. Particularly harmful are:

Chronic stress

Poor Sleep

Obesity

Lack of a balanced diet

Exposure to endocrine-disrupting chemicals (e.g., BPA, phthalates)

Alternatively, balanced, regular resistance training and a healthy diet can enhance AR expression and sensitivity, which can contribute to better health and performance.

Medical Conditions and Genetic Variations

Some conditions, such as Androgen Insensitivity Syndrome (AIS), prevent the body from responding properly to androgens, either partially or completely. (5) AIS is caused by mutations in the androgen receptor (AR) gene and may result in varying degrees of impaired male sexual development despite normal or elevated testosterone levels.

Beyond AIS, certain genetic mutations or chronic illnesses may also disrupt androgen receptor signaling, leading to a variety of hormonal imbalances. These may include:

Polymorphisms in the AR gene

Kennedy’s Disease

Why Androgen Receptors Matter for Men’s Health

Healthy androgen receptor function is a critical factor for men in muscle, sexual health, and overall metabolic health. Optimizing AR function can improve strength, libido, and metabolic processes that influence weight loss and cardiovascular health.

Maintain Lean Muscle and Physical Performance

Strong androgen receptor function is closely tied to improved strength gains, faster recovery, and better overall physical performance. Testosterone’s effects on muscle protein synthesis are primarily mediated through ARs, making them essential for muscle and performance optimization.

Athletes and fitness enthusiasts often focus on enhancing AR density and sensitivity by following targeted exercise regimens, proper nutrition, and adequate rest to maximize gains in strength and muscle mass. Research suggests that higher AR density in muscle tissues enhances the body’s ability to respond to testosterone, improving muscle hypertrophy and endurance. (6)

Sexual Health and Libido

Androgen receptors are crucial in sexual health, influencing libido, erection quality, and reproductive function.

Testosterone, through AR activation, contributes to sexual desire and fertility. Low AR expression or dysfunction may manifest as decreased sexual desire, erectile dysfunction, or lower sperm count, which are commonly seen in men with low testosterone levels or impaired androgen signaling. (7)

In some cases, AR mutations (such as those found in AIS) can result in complete androgen resistance, leading to infertility despite normal testosterone production.

Metabolic and Cardiovascular Impact

Optimizing androgen receptor signaling also has significant implications for metabolic health and cardiovascular function. ARs regulate several metabolic processes, including fat distribution, insulin sensitivity, and cholesterol levels.

Men with well-functioning ARs often exhibit healthier fat distribution (lower visceral fat) and improved insulin sensitivity, both of which are crucial for managing body weight and preventing metabolic syndrome. Research has shown that AR activation helps regulate glucose metabolism, reduce fat storage, and improve lipid profiles, which can lower the risk of cardiovascular diseases like atherosclerosis and hypertension. (8)
